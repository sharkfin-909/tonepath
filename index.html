Build your ear, guided by visual patterns

Tonepath lays out each chord's guide tones and passing notes as a honeycomb of highlighted cells, that becomes a map that you navigate. Use the app to enter or select a chord progression, then use the hexagonal grid to create your own exercise, guided by the highlighted cells. Then play your etude on your instrument and expand your musical vocabulary.

Free. No account. Runs in your browser.

Voice leading principles gamified

You enter notes of a selected length in free time. As you enter the last note of a chord, the new chord is applied to the grid and you are guided by the pattern.

  • Steps run to the right, thirds run up-left. So a chord’s tones sit in a tight cluster, and the guide tones (the 3rds and 7ths that carry the harmony) are always close at hand.
  • Every note is coloured by its degree. Root, third, fifth and seventh each keep their colour from chord to chord, so you can watch what changes when the harmony moves.
  • The grid changes with the chord. As a ii–V–I goes by, the grid reshapes itself for each chord, and you see which notes stay and which move.

What you can do with it

  1. Edit Progression

    Type chord symbols into a bar grid. Tonepath suggests chords that belong to the key, but you may choose any chord. Each chord gets the standard scale, which you may change. If the scale is a mode, the chord gets marked with it's key family.

  2. Improvise on the grid

    You can play over the form by by picking notes on the grid with the mouse, but it's more comfortable to move the mouse and press the A key to trigger a note. Chord bump can move to the next chord after a set number of notes, so you work through the changes at your own pace. Alternatively you can use the metronome. Both ways the chord will be played as accompaniment (you may also select just bass or root).

  3. Write an etude

    Compose a melody over the changes using the grid. Your etude is written out as standard notation as you go. As in improvising, you may use chord bump or metronome setting.

  4. Tune and warm up

    Using your device's microphone, get in tune with the tuner and try playing into Tonepath. The grid will react chromatically.

  5. Improvise on your instrument

    Play your instrument into the chart. The grid will react to the currently selected chord, and you may explore the tonality by stepping through the chords, or play along with the progression with a metronome. The cells of the scale notes you are playing will light up as you play, via the microphone.

  6. Read your etude

    Play it on your instrument from one page with a metronome and accompaniment, with a moving playhead. Use a tablet on your music stand if you like.

Made for jazz students and anyone learning to improvise

  • Your instrument’s key. Etudes show in written pitch for B♭ trumpet, clarinet and tenor sax, E♭ alto and baritone sax, F horn, trombone, guitar, bass, flute, violin, viola, cello and vibraphone.
  • Loop what’s hard. Pick the bars you’re stuck on and loop them at your own tempo.
  • Hear the harmony. Each chord can sound as it arrives, as a chord, its root or its bass note.
  • Start from the classics. Built-in progressions include the 12-bar blues, a jazz blues, a minor blues, rhythm changes in B♭, a I–vi–ii–V turnaround and a Dorian vamp. Make your own copy of any of them, or write your own.

Free, private, open source

  • No account. Your progressions and etudes are saved in your browser, and you can export them to a file and import them anywhere.
  • Nothing is recorded. The microphone only listens while you play, to light up the grid.
  • Open source. The code is on GitHub. Built by Ben Amato at Truelathe.

Questions

Is Tonepath free?

Yes, completely. There’s no account, no trial and no ads.

What do I need?

A modern browser. It works best on a laptop or desktop, reads well on a tablet, and is usable on a phone. The microphone features need your browser’s permission.

Does it work with my instrument?

Any instrument works with the microphone, one note at a time. Etudes can be shown in written pitch for the common transposing instruments (B♭, E♭ and F), as well as for concert-pitch instruments.

Can I use the changes to a jazz standard?

Yes. Type in any chord progression you like, and Tonepath gives each chord its scale.

Where are my etudes saved?

In your browser, on this device. Export them to a file to back them up or move them to another computer.
